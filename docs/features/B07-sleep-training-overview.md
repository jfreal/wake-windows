# Sleep-Training Methods Overview (Neutral)

**Competitor verdict:** Table-stakes-ish — overviews exist, but a genuinely neutral, cited, non-judgmental treatment that also corrects the cortisol-harm myth is uncommon.

## Problem / user need
Sleep training is one of the most polarizing, guilt-laden topics in early parenting. Parents want to understand their options and whether the science supports harm claims, without being pushed toward or shamed for any choice. They need a calm, cited, method-agnostic reference.

## What users actually say
Parents feel judged from every direction and are frightened by the widely-shared "cortisol harms your baby" narrative. The brand is reassure, don't grade — this is exactly where a non-judgmental, evidence-first tone earns trust.

## Competitor comparison
Apps and blogs cover methods, but many lean toward a house philosophy or amplify the cortisol fear. A neutral menu of methods plus an honest read of the efficacy and safety evidence is differentiated by tone and transparency.

## Our approach (spec)
A neutral, inclusive overview:
- **Methods, described plainly, no ranking:** unmodified extinction ("cry it out"); graduated extinction (Ferber / timed check-ins); bedtime fading / positive routines; scheduled awakenings; chair method / camping out; parent education (prevention).
- **Efficacy (Tier 1):** AASM-commissioned review (Mindell 2006, 52 studies) — 94% found behavioral interventions efficacious; >80% of treated children showed clinically significant, durable (3–6 mo) improvement. Consistent bedtime routine (Mindell RCT, 405 pairs) reduces sleep-onset latency and night wakings, dose-dependently.
- **Long-term safety (Tier 1):** Price et al. 2012, 5-year follow-up (326 children) — no differences vs. controls on emotional/behavioral problems, sleep, cortisol, attachment, or parent mental health.
- **The cortisol concern, in context:** the widely-shared Middlemiss 2012 study had only 25 infants, no control group, no baseline, 2 days of data — too weak to support harm claims.
- **Readiness:** not appropriate under ~4 months; evidence base is ~4–6 months+. Framed as one option among many; "no wrong loving choice."

Every claim carries a **Tier badge + citation** (B01).

## Scope — MVP
Static overview: method descriptions, T1 efficacy + safety, the Middlemiss context, readiness note. Neutral tone, tier badges + citations.

## Scope — later
Optional method-comparison table; links to the troubleshooter (B05) and bedtime-routine guidance; "not now" path for parents who don't want to train.

## Edge cases & gotchas
- Never prescribe or pressure; present all methods and the choice not to train as valid.
- Correct the cortisol myth without dismissing parents' emotional concerns.
- Keep the under-4-month readiness caveat prominent; pair with safe sleep (B03).

## Evidence & citations
Research 02 §9 (Tier 1): Mindell 2006 (PubMed 17068979) efficacy; Mindell RCT bedtime routines (PMC4402657); Price et al. 2012 (Pediatrics) 5-year safety; Middlemiss 2012 critique (Jolly Good Sleep; Betteroo audit). Readiness ~4–6 mo+ per research 02 §9.

## Effort
Low. Static content + citation data; tone-sensitive editing.

## Risks / open questions
Maintaining strict neutrality; how much method detail before it reads as endorsement; ensuring inclusive tone for non-training families.

## Success metric
Overview open rate; citation-open rate; absence of "pushy/judgy" complaints; qualitative "finally a balanced take" feedback.
