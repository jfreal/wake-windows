# AAP Safe-Sleep Reference Panel

**Competitor verdict:** Partial — Pampers/Smart Sleep Coach surfaces safe sleep; most schedulers omit it. A credible sleep app cannot.

## Problem / user need
Roughly 3,500 U.S. infants die suddenly during sleep each year (SIDS + accidental suffocation/strangulation). A sleep app that schedules naps but never states the AAP safe-sleep basics is not credible. Parents need the current consensus, calmly presented, where they already are.

## What users actually say
Parents want authoritative, non-alarmist guidance and distrust apps that feel like they're guessing. Anxiety-inducing, scolding copy is a documented turn-off ("feel like you're not doing enough"); the brand promise is reassure, don't grade.

## Competitor comparison
Pampers' Smart Sleep Coach includes safe-sleep content. Most wake-window schedulers (and pure trackers) do not surface it prominently. Persistent, cited safe-sleep is a low-cost credibility win most rivals skip.

## Our approach (spec)
A persistent, always-accessible **Tier 1** reference panel summarizing AAP 2022, informational not alarmist:
- **Back to sleep**, every sleep, until age 1.
- **Firm, flat, non-inclined surface** (CPSC-compliant crib/bassinet/play yard; fitted sheet only). Loungers/nests/inclined sleepers are not for sleep.
- **Room-share (separate surface) for ≥6 months** — up to ~50% SIDS reduction.
- **No loose bedding / soft objects** — no pillows, blankets, bumpers, stuffed toys, weighted products.
- **No bed-sharing;** never sofa/armchair (risk up to ~67×).
- Supporting: pacifier at sleep, avoid overheating, no smoke exposure, supervised awake tummy time; stop swaddling at first signs of rolling (~3–4 mo); home monitors not recommended for SIDS.

Nap-specific essentials, added from the 2026-08 FAQ sweep (research 07 §15) because the nap-time questions parents actually ask were the ones the panel didn't answer:
- **Naps too:** if baby falls asleep in a car seat, swing, or carrier, move them to a firm, flat surface as soon as practical.
- **Never doze off with baby on a couch or armchair** — the riskiest arrangement of all. If you might drift, put baby down first.

Every item carries a **T1 badge + AAP 2022 citation** (B01). Tone: factual, calm, no guilt.

The same hard rule is restated in the **contact-naps panel**, where the question actually arises: a contact nap needs an awake adult, and a planned nap in the crib is far safer than an accidental nap in a recliner. It is deliberately styled in the calm slate register rather than amber — this is load-bearing safety information, not a scold — and it sits *after* the reassurance that contact naps are normal and not a bad habit, so the safety line lands without turning the whole subject into a warning.

## Scope — MVP
Static panel, reachable from home and from any nap/bedtime view. Tier badges + AAP citation. Plain, non-alarmist copy. Accessible / dark-room legible.

## Scope — later
Age-aware emphasis (e.g. surface rolling-and-swaddle note ~3–4 mo, room-share reminder near 6 mo); links to HealthyChildren.org parent guide; short "why" expanders.

## Edge cases & gotchas
- Never imply blame or that following the app guarantees safety.
- Bed-sharing families: state AAP position factually without shaming; point to risk-reduction guidance, not judgment.
- Keep informational, not a substitute for a pediatrician; add that line.

## Evidence & citations
Tier 1: AAP 2022 Policy — Sleep-Related Infant Deaths (Pediatrics, e2022057990) and Technical Report (e2022057991); HealthyChildren.org Safe Sleep parent guide; Cribs for Kids AAP 2022 summary. All figures (~3,500 deaths/yr; ~50% room-share reduction; ~67× sofa risk) per research 02 §8.

## Effort
Low. Static, cited content; must stay current with AAP updates.

## Risks / open questions
Tone calibration (informational vs. scary); how much detail before overload; monitoring AAP for policy revisions.

## Success metric
Panel reach/visibility (always ≤1 tap); open rate; absence of "preachy/alarmist" complaints; presence as a cited trust signal.
