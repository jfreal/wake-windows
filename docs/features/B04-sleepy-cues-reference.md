# Sleepy-Cues Reference

**Competitor verdict:** Partial — cue lists exist elsewhere, but none pair them with the honest "cues over clock under ~9 months" framing that fits our brand.

## Problem / user need
A clock-only schedule fails when parents can't read the baby. Cues are the most reliable signal in early infancy, and reading early cues (not late/overtired ones) is what prevents the overtired spiral. Parents need a quick, trustworthy reference for what to watch for — and when cues matter more than the clock.

## What users actually say
Parents describe missing the window and getting a "wired," hard-to-settle baby; they want help distinguishing overtired resistance from a too-short window. Rigid countdowns cause anxiety — the ask is for interpretation, not a stopwatch.

## Competitor comparison
Cue content appears in blogs (Taking Cara Babies, Cleveland Clinic, Huckleberry) and some apps, but competitors rarely tie cues to age-based reliability or to a cues-vs-clock mode. This pairs with A04 to frame it in a way no competitor does.

## Our approach (spec)
A scannable reference with three states, each with concrete signs:
- **Early ("catch it now"):** slowing/less activity, glazed staring, droopy eyelids, breaking eye contact, red/pink brow, less interest in play. Ideal moment to start settling.
- **Late / overtired:** yawning and eye-rubbing (often late), fussiness → frantic crying, arching away, clinginess, hard to settle. Note the cortisol/adrenaline "second wind."
- **Undertired (window too short):** happy babbling/playing in crib, only a short catnap, calm resistance — distinct from overtired resistance.

**Reliability note, prominent:** cues are most reliable **under ~9 months**; after that, shift toward age-appropriate schedules. Cleveland Clinic tip: note the clock time cues appear, then settle ~5 min before that next time. T3 badge + citation.

## Scope — MVP
Static three-state reference with the under-9-month reliability note and the "settle 5 min before" tip. Tier badge + citations. Accessible, dark-room legible.

## Scope — later
Contextual surfacing inside the schedule near a predicted window; link to A04 cues-vs-clock mode; short illustrations of each cue.

## Edge cases & gotchas
- Emphasize early over late cues — yawning is often already late.
- Don't present cues as infallible; older babies mask them.
- Keep undertired vs. overtired distinction clear to avoid the wrong fix.

## Evidence & citations
Tier 3 (clinically endorsed), research 01 §6: Taking Cara Babies (newborn sleepy cues), Cleveland Clinic (15 signs / note-the-time tip), Huckleberry & Healthline (overtired), Pathways.org (sleep cues). Reliability-under-9-months and "watch the baby, not the clock, especially under 9 months" per research 01 §7.
